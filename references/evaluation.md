# Final findings and their limits

The strongest retained final tactic was one coherent GPT-6 Luna Max owner
followed by a fresh GPT-6 Sol High finish-and-repair pass. On two bounded tasks,
it reduced estimated API cost by **30.7–34.8%** against fresh GPT-6 Sol High
direct controls, with equal final **100/100** quality scores. It took
**34.3–95.6% longer model-stage time**.

These are selected final **workflow diagnostics**, not executions of the
shipping automatic-routing skill text, not an overall study effect, and not a
general Orchestrator-versus-direct win. The automatic policy is a synthesis of
those findings and routing research. Its performance has not yet been measured.

## Apples-to-apples scope

Each tactic had its own fresh matched direct control. Both arms got the same
fresh Sol High finishing opportunity, including repair, then separate objective
grading and a blind Sol High quality score. Cases were prepared by Sol XHigh;
checks and grading answers were withheld from solvers. Final acceptance included
critical checks, semantic review and zero severe defects on these diagnostics.

Arm costs include routing, solving/integration, repairs and equal finishing.
Shared preparation and independent grading are excluded from the arm comparison.
Time covers model stages, not environment setup, independent grading or human
waits. A direct workflow without a finishing pass could cost less than this
baseline; these numbers do not quantify that comparison.

## What the policy takes from the evidence

- One coherent cheaper owner can save money despite processing more tokens.
- Ownership boundaries and verification coverage matter more than agent counts.
- A review must be able to repair, but even capable finishing is not a guarantee.
- Preserve working behaviour and rerun affected checks after repair.
- Small or context-coupled work often cannot repay handoffs; keep it direct.
- Do not convert a study's quality tolerance into permission to miss a real
  user's acceptance requirements.

## Accounting and pricing

The retained research/testing/preparation/grading audit records **345,097,261
tokens across 426 deduplicated complete calls**: 341,131,707 input and 3,965,554
output. Cached input (322,719,488) is part of input; reasoning output (1,991,356)
is part of output. Uncached input is 18,412,219.

This is the auditable retained total across the research programme, not an exact
lifetime total. It excludes the main conversation, absent archives, incomplete
receipts and unmetered engineering reviews. It already includes the final study;
do not add a final-study subtotal again.

The retained calls total approximately **$127.86 API-equivalent** under the
frozen 2026-09-23 rate table and recorded cache mix. This is not a Codex Pro
subscription charge or a current-price quote. Cost comparisons are sensitive to
provider rates, cache accounting and actual model identity.

[Public aggregate data](../docs/results.json) contains the final two diagnostics
and aggregate accounting only. Raw private traces and intermediate experiments
are deliberately not distributed. Consequently, this public summary alone is
not a fully independently reproducible benchmark release.

## Results are not guaranteed

Savings, quality and speed vary by workload, task topology, initiating model,
worker/finisher choice, harness, effort support, warm context, cache reuse,
tooling, latency and acceptance coverage. Two diagnostics are a small sample,
not statistical evidence of broad generalisation. A 100/100 score reflects the
checks used, not proof that no bug can exist.

Some broader compatibility fixtures depended on packages unavailable in the
evaluation environment, so not every upstream root fixture was exercised.
The automatic policy's offline tests check packaging and runner contracts, not
new comparative performance.

Measured solver evidence is primarily GPT-6. **Claude-family and GPT-6.1 solver
evaluations are backlogged and unmeasured**, even if those models can host the
skill today. Do not apply GPT-6 numerical savings to them.
